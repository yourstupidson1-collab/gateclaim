## What this kit does
Helps you file the right letter with the right attachments. It does **not** collect money for you and it does **not** promise a number.

## Which track are you on?

### Track A — US itinerary (to, from, or within the United States)
You are usually entitled to a **refund of the fare** (taxes + unused extras) if the airline **cancelled** or **significantly delayed/changed** the flight and **you choose not to travel**.

DOT’s refund page: https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds

A “significant” schedule change includes departing **3+ hours** earlier/later on a **domestic** itinerary, or **6+ hours** on an **international** one (Refund Rules / FAA Reauthorization 2024).

If you **take** the delayed flight, a refund of the fare is generally **not** owed. You may still ask the airline for **reasonable expenses** it promised in its customer service plan.

**Denied boarding (bumped, you did not volunteer)** on an oversold flight can mean cash. For transportation on/after 22 Jan 2025, published **caps** are **$1,075** (200% of fare) or **$2,150** (400% of fare) depending on how late the substitute flight arrives. Source: 14 CFR 250.5 / 89 FR 84818. Zero if they get you there within about an hour.

US delays are **not** automatic €600. Do not write that in a US letter.

### Track B — EU / EEA / Swiss rights (Regulation 261/2004)
May apply if the flight is intra-EU, leaves the EU, or arrives in the EU on an EU carrier. Official: https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm

Cash compensation **if you qualify** (not extraordinary circumstances the airline can prove):

| Amount | Distance (typical) |
|---|---|
| €250 | 1 500 km or less |
| €400 | 1 500–3 500 km / longer intra-EU |
| €600 | more than 3 500 km |

Arrival delay **3 hours+** can qualify. Extraordinary circumstances (weather, ATC, some strikes) can block cash even when assistance is still owed. Always file with the **operating** airline first.

## Proof checklist (attach, don’t invent)
- Booking code / e-ticket
- Boarding pass or app screenshot
- Delay/cancel notice (email/SMS/app)
- Actual departure and arrival times (FlightAware / airport board photo)
- Receipts: hotel, meals, ground transport you paid because they didn’t help
- Names of agents you spoke with + times
- What they offered (voucher vs refund) and what you **refused**

## Deadline calendar (start today)
- **Day 0:** Save every screenshot. Do not accept a voucher if you want a refund.
- **Day 1:** Send letter 1 to the airline (template below).
- **Day 14:** If no useful reply, send a follow-up.
- **Day 30–60:** US — DOT consumer complaint: https://secure.dot.gov/air-travel-complaint  
  EU — national enforcement body in the country of the incident after ~2 months / unsatisfactory reply.
- **Bags:** EU written claim in **7 days** (damage) or **21 days** (delay) of getting the bag.

## Letter 1 — US refund (cancel / significant change)
```
Subject: Refund request — [airline] [flight] [date] — record locator [PNR]

I held a ticket on [flight] from [A] to [B] on [date], record locator [PNR].

The flight was [cancelled / delayed or changed by __ hours]. I did not accept rebooking, a voucher, or other compensation. I want a full refund of the airfare, taxes, and unused ancillary fees to the original form of payment.

This follows DOT refund rules for cancelled or significantly delayed/changed flights:
https://www.transportation.gov/individuals/aviation-consumer-protection/refunds

Attachments: ticket, delay notice, receipts (if any).

Name:
Phone:
Email:
Mailing address:
```

## Letter 2 — US denied boarding (involuntary)
```
Subject: Denied boarding compensation — [flight] [date] — [PNR]

I was involuntarily denied boarding on [flight] [date] [PNR]. I did not volunteer my seat. Alternate arrival was [time], which is [__] after the original arrival.

Please pay denied boarding compensation under 14 CFR part 250 and refund unused ancillary fees.

Attachments: boarding pass, bump notice, substitute itinerary.
```

## Letter 3 — EU261 compensation (only if Track B applies)
```
Subject: Regulation 261/2004 claim — [flight] [date] — [PNR]

I request the compensation, refund/re-routing choice, and assistance due under Regulation (EC) 261/2004.

Flight: [number]  [A] → [B]  [date]
Scheduled arrival: [ ]
Actual arrival: [ ]
Delay at destination: [ ] hours
Distance (as published): [ ] km

I was [cancelled / delayed 3h+ / denied boarding]. I have not received benefits for this disruption under another country’s rules.

If you claim extraordinary circumstances, send the evidence.

Attachments: ticket, boarding pass, delay notice, receipts.

Official summary: https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm
```

## Letter 4 — expense reimbursement
```
Subject: Reimbursement of disruption expenses — [flight] [date] — [PNR]

You did not provide [meal / hotel / ground transport]. I paid $[ ] for necessary, reasonable costs while waiting. Receipts attached. Please reimburse to the original payment method.
```
