Flight cancelled refund: what you can actually claim

If the airline cancelled your flight, start here. A US refund of the ticket is not EU 261 cash. Then fill the letter and send it to the airline tonight.

Make my refund letter

Delayed, not cancelled? Flight delayed refund.

United States

On a flight to, from, or within the United States, if the airline cancelled it and you choose not to travel — you decline the rebooking, the voucher, and other compensation — you are generally entitled to a refund of the airfare, taxes, and unused extras to the original form of payment. A voucher you didn’t ask for is not a refund.

That refund does not depend on the airline being at fault. Weather can still mean a refund if you don’t take the alternative. DOT’s page: transportation.gov — Refunds (14 CFR part 260). When the airline is the merchant of record, the refund is supposed to be prompt: 7 business days for a credit card, 20 calendar days for cash, check, debit, or another form of payment. If a ticket agent sold the ticket, the refund often has to go through that agent.

If you accept the new flight and travel, a fare refund is usually not owed. You can still ask for expenses the airline promised in its customer service plan.

A US cancellation is not automatic €250, €400, or €600. That fixed cash is an EU rule. Involuntary denied-boarding cash under 14 CFR part 250 is a different event — you were bumped — not a cancellation.

EU / EEA / Switzerland

Regulation 261/2004 can apply if the flight is covered (it departs the EU/EEA, or it arrives there on an EU/EEA carrier). Official: Your Europe — air passenger rights.

On a cancellation you generally choose: a refund of the unused ticket (the regulation says within 7 days), re-routing as soon as possible, or re-routing on a later date. Care — meals, and a hotel plus transport if you are stranded overnight — can still be owed while you wait.

Cash on top of that choice is separate. Article 7 amounts are €250 / €400 / €600 by distance, and only if you qualify. It is not owed when the airline told you at least 14 days before departure, or told you later and offered a re-route inside the regulation’s windows (between 7 and 14 days: leave no more than 2 hours early and arrive less than 4 hours late; under 7 days: leave no more than 1 hour early and arrive less than 2 hours late). It is also not owed when the airline proves extraordinary circumstances it could not avoid. If you take the re-route and still arrive inside the shorter windows in Article 7, that cash can be cut in half. A ticket refund can still be due when that cash is not. Switzerland follows a parallel passenger-rights rule; it is not the US fare-refund rule.

Do this tonight

  1. Screenshot the cancellation notice and your booking.
  2. Keep receipts if you paid for a hotel, meal, or ride they didn’t provide.
  3. Fill the letter — pick Cancelled, then US refund or EU 261. Do not use the US bump letter for a cancellation.
  4. Send it to the operating airline. Do not accept a voucher if you want a refund.
